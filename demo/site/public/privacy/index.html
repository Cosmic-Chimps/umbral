Umbral demo

What happens to your data

This is a demo of a sign-in server. If you sign in, here is what it keeps, for how long, and who else sees anything.

What is kept

When you sign in with GitHub, Google or Discord
What the provider shares after you consent: your id at the provider, your name, your email address (and whether the provider says it is verified) and the address of your avatar. Umbral asks for nothing else and never sees your password.
When you sign in with a Mastodon or GoToSocial account
Your account's id on your server, its username and display name, and your avatar's address. Umbral reads nothing you post.
When you sign in with a passkey
The passkey's public key and the name you give it. The private half never leaves your device, and there is no password at all.
In your browser
Umbral sets a cookie that keeps you signed in to it, one that remembers which way in you used last, and one for the theme if you pick one. The sample app sets a session cookie and keeps nothing on its side.
A record of sign-ins
Umbral's security log notes each sign-in: the time, the way in, the app, your user id here, and the IP address you came from. The server in front keeps no access log.

For how long

Every night at 04:00 UTC every account is deleted, yours included, with its sessions and tokens, and so is Umbral's security log, except one line saying that the clearing happened. The instance keeps running while it does. If a night's clearing fails, the people who run the demo are told, and it happens at the next one that succeeds.

The only copy of the instance is a backup taken by hand while it was empty, so that it can be rebuilt. No copy is taken on a schedule, so no copy holds an account.

What Umbral and the sample write to their console while they run (start-up lines, warnings, request paths; no account data and no addresses) is kept by the host by size, about 100 MB per service, not by time. The nightly clearing does not touch it.

What your own server sees

When you sign in with a Mastodon or GoToSocial account, Umbral registers an application named Umbral on your server the first time anyone from that server signs in, asking for the read:accounts permission and giving this demo's address as its website. Your server lists it under the applications you have authorised, and you can revoke it there at any time. The nightly clearing keeps that registration, so it is made once per server, not once per night.

What is not done

  • Nothing is shared with anyone, sold, or used for anything other than showing you the sign-in.
  • Nothing is analysed. These pages run no script and load nothing from another host; the sign-in pages and the sample app load nothing from another host either.
  • No email is sent.

Who runs it

Cosmic Chimps, the people behind Umbral, on a server of their own. For a question, open an issue on Umbral's repository. To have your data removed before the next reset, write to hello@cosmic-chimps.com, so that you need not say in public that you signed in.
